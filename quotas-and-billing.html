Quotas & billing

Godzilla operates on a tiered plan system. Each plan includes a monthly request quota and a per-minute rate limit. When you exceed your plan's limits, the API begins returning 429 Too Many Requests responses.

Plan tiers

The following table shows the request quota and rate limit for each plan tier:

Plan Monthly Quota Rate Limit Features
Free 10,000 requests 100 req/min Community support, single workspace
Standard 1,000,000 requests 1,000 req/min Email support, team access, webhooks
Professional 10,000,000 requests 5,000 req/min Priority support, audit logs, SSO
Enterprise Custom Custom Dedicated support, SLA, on-premise options

All rate limits are per workspace, not per API key. If your team shares a workspace, the rate limit applies to the aggregate request volume across all keys.

Rate limits

Each API response includes rate-limit headers that tell you your current quota status:

  • X-RateLimit-Limit — Your per-minute request allowance.
  • X-RateLimit-Remaining — Requests remaining in this minute.
  • X-RateLimit-Reset — Unix timestamp when the window resets.

When a request would exceed your per-minute limit, the server responds with 429 Too Many Requests and a Retry-After header indicating how many seconds to wait before retrying. Respect this header — retrying before the window resets will continue to fail.

rate-limit-headers.txt text 
X-RateLimit-Limit: 1000
X-RateLimit-Remaining: 847
X-RateLimit-Reset: 1704067200

Monthly quota

Beyond the per-minute rate limit, each plan includes a monthly request quota. The quota resets on the first day of each month (UTC). Your plan tier determines your monthly allowance.

You can monitor your monthly usage in the workspace settings dashboard. There's no 429 response for exceeding monthly quota — instead, your workspace becomes quota_exceeded and all API requests fail with 403 Forbidden. Read the error body for instructions on how to upgrade or purchase additional quota.

Quota exceeded blocks the entire workspace

Once monthly quota is exceeded, all API operations halt until your quota resets or your plan tier increases. This includes reads, writes, and webhook deliveries. Plan accordingly and monitor usage during high-traffic periods.

What happens when you exceed your quota

Per-minute rate limit (429)

If you send more than your plan's per-minute limit in a single calendar minute, the excess requests receive a 429 Too Many Requests response. The API is still functional — you can retry after the window resets or after the time specified in Retry-After.

How to recover: Implement exponential backoff with jitter. Read the Retry-After header and wait at least that many seconds before retrying. If you regularly hit the rate limit, consider upgrading your plan tier or caching API responses.

Monthly quota exceeded (403)

Once your workspace's aggregate monthly request count reaches your plan's quota, all subsequent requests return 403 Forbidden with the error code quota_exceeded:

quota-exceeded-error.json json 
{
  "error": {
    "code": "quota_exceeded",
    "message": "Monthly request quota exhausted. Upgrade your plan or purchase additional quota.",
    "request_id": "req_abc123"
  }
}

How to recover: Upgrade to a higher plan tier or purchase additional monthly quota. Only workspace admins can manage billing and upgrades — visit the Godzilla web console and navigate to Settings > Billing. The quota resets automatically on the first day of the next month.

Upgrading and custom quotas

To upgrade your plan or request a custom quota, log in to the Godzilla web console:

  1. Navigate to Settings > Billing.
  2. Review your current plan and monthly usage.
  3. Select a higher tier or contact sales for a custom arrangement.

Enterprise plans and on-premise deployments are handled by our sales team — email sales@godzilladocs.dev with details about your use case and expected monthly volume.

Usage monitoring

Set up workspace alerts to notify you when your monthly usage approaches 80% of your quota. This gives you time to upgrade or adjust your integration before hitting the hard limit.
